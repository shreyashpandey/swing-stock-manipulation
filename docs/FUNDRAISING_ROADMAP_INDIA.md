# SwingDesk Fundraising Roadmap For India

Last updated: 2026-08-03
Status: working plan

## Purpose

This document explains how to connect with Indian investors for SwingDesk, including:

- who to target first
- how to use Startup India programs
- what materials to prepare
- what milestones to hit before outreach
- how to run a 12-week fundraising process

This is designed for the current SwingDesk strategy:

- consumer / prosumer investing analytics
- subscription product
- no SEBI-registered adviser or research analyst attached at present
- positioned as analytics software, not advisory

## Fundraising Positioning

Before contacting investors, the company story needs to be simple.

### Recommended category

Position SwingDesk as:

- wealthtech
- consumer fintech
- investing infrastructure for self-directed retail users
- analytics subscription software

### Recommended one-line pitch

> SwingDesk is a research and analytics workbench for Indian active investors, built around unusual-activity detection, screening, portfolio diagnostics, and backtesting for self-directed decision-making.

### Avoid pitching it as

- a broker
- a stock tips product
- an advisory firm
- a "calls" business
- a managed-trading system

Investors will immediately ask about regulation if the pitch sounds like advisory.

## Who To Reach Out To

Fundraising should happen in layers, not all at once.

### Tier 1: Angel investors and operator-angels

Start here.

Best fit:

- founders or early operators from Indian fintech, broking, payments, wealthtech, and consumer internet
- angels with a history of fintech or consumer-tech bets

Why this is the best first step:

- they understand trust-heavy fintech UX
- they can help with positioning and distribution
- they are more tolerant of an early product than large funds
- they are more likely to give tactical feedback and warm introductions

Examples of useful backgrounds:

- broking / investing apps
- payments
- consumer subscription products
- growth and product leaders in Indian fintech

### Tier 2: Early-stage India funds

Reach these after the deck, product narrative, and early traction are stronger.

Target funds with one or more of these theses:

- fintech
- wealthtech
- consumer internet
- AI + workflow software
- India-first mass-market software

Relevant firms to research and potentially target:

- Peak XV / Surge
- Lightspeed
- Z47
- Prime Venture Partners
- Antler India
- Y Combinator

The point is not to spam these firms immediately. The point is to build qualified intros into the partners most likely to care about:

- retail investor behavior in India
- subscription software
- new wealthtech behavior
- AI-assisted consumer products

### Tier 3: Incubators and accelerators

Useful if:

- you are still refining the company story
- you want structured mentorship
- you want investor access
- you need help with founder network, GTM, and fundraising readiness

Prioritize:

- Startup India ecosystem channels
- Surge / Peak XV
- Antler India
- Y Combinator

## Startup India Path

Startup India probably will not directly fund your ad campaigns, but it can help meaningfully with:

- credibility
- discoverability
- mentoring
- investor access
- ecosystem introductions
- possible grant/seed support through schemes or incubators

### Step 1: Get DPIIT recognition

This should be done first if eligible.

Why it matters:

- credibility in investor conversations
- access to Startup India programs
- easier entry into ecosystem workflows
- useful for awards, schemes, and recognition layers

Important current note:

- Startup India states the turnover threshold for recognition was revised to `₹200 crore` through a DPIIT notification dated `February 4, 2026`

### Step 2: Create BHASKAR profile

BHASKAR is Startup India's ecosystem registry and networking layer.

Use it for:

- founder discoverability
- investor visibility
- ecosystem searchability
- profile credibility

Action:

- create founder/startup profile
- complete the profile fully
- make the startup description crisp and investor-readable
- add product category, sector, website, and traction notes

### Step 3: Apply to MAARG mentorship

MAARG is the Startup India mentorship platform.

Use it for:

- pitch feedback
- fintech/consumer GTM advice
- legal/compliance introductions
- fundraising readiness

Action:

- apply as a startup
- specifically seek mentors in:
  - fintech
  - consumer subscription products
  - growth
  - fundraising
  - regulatory product design

### Step 4: Use Startup India Investor Connect

Treat this as an access channel, not your only fundraising engine.

Use it for:

- investor visibility
- supplementary pipeline
- credibility signaling once the deck is clean

### Step 5: Track awards and programs

National Startup Awards can matter for:

- visibility
- investor attention
- government and ecosystem introductions
- credibility

As of the currently visible Startup India pages, application states and timelines can be mixed across pages, so verify the live cycle before planning around it.

### Step 6: Track seed and incubator schemes

The Startup India Seed Fund Scheme explicitly supports:

- proof of concept
- prototype development
- product trials
- market entry
- commercialization

But the public Seed Fund portal currently shows a final notice with:

- startup application last date: `May 31, 2026`
- incubator selection completion: `June 30, 2026`

Because today is `August 3, 2026`, assume that round is closed unless the portal reopens or a new cycle is announced.

Even so, relevant incubators and state startup schemes may still be available and should be tracked in parallel.

## What To Prepare Before Outreach

Do not start fundraising before these materials are ready.

### 1. Pitch deck

Keep it to 10-12 slides.

Core sections:

- problem
- why now
- product
- why users care
- business model
- market
- differentiation
- traction
- go-to-market
- compliance posture
- roadmap
- ask

### 2. One-page memo

Useful for angels who do not want a full deck first.

Include:

- what SwingDesk is
- for whom
- what makes it different
- current traction
- what you are raising
- why this can become large

### 3. Product demo

Required.

Best format:

- 2-3 minute Loom
- one investor demo account or video
- clear walkthrough of the hero use case

### 4. Data room

At minimum:

- deck
- company incorporation docs
- cap table
- financial model
- product roadmap
- KPI snapshot
- legal/compliance note on tool-not-advice posture

### 5. KPI sheet

Even if small, track:

- signups
- active users
- retention
- watchlists created
- scans run
- alerts saved
- paid conversion if any
- revenue if any

## What Investors Will Ask

Prepare for these questions:

### Product

- Why is this better than Tickertape, Trendlyne, Chartink, or StockEdge?
- What is the single most valuable wedge?
- Why will users come back daily or weekly?

### Market

- Who is the core user?
- What % of Indian retail investors could realistically pay?
- Is this for traders, long-term investors, or both?

### Business model

- Why will users pay `₹2,999/year` or more?
- What is the free-to-paid conversion story?
- What is the long-term ARPU upside?

### Compliance

- How do you stay out of advisory territory?
- Why won’t SEBI risk block growth?
- What exactly is the legal posture today?

### Distribution

- How will you acquire the first 1,000 users?
- What is the cheapest channel?
- What makes CAC work?

### Moat

- Why can’t a bigger player copy this?
- What proprietary workflow, data combination, or user behavior advantage can you build?

## Best Outreach Narrative

For now, do not pitch "all-in-one stock market app."

Pitch:

**India's unusual-activity and swing research workbench for self-directed investors**

This is better because it:

- creates sharper differentiation
- sounds like software, not advisory
- makes monetization more believable
- gives investors one strong reason to remember the company

## 12-Week Roadmap

### Weeks 1-2: Preparation

- finalize fundraising story
- finish deck
- record demo
- clean compliant positioning
- create investor CRM sheet
- apply for DPIIT recognition if not already done
- create BHASKAR profile

### Weeks 3-4: Validation and warm-up

- talk to 10-15 mentors/operators first
- use MAARG if available
- refine deck from feedback
- prepare short memo and founder intro blurb
- identify 40-60 target investors

### Weeks 5-6: Warm intros first

- reach out to founder friends, operators, and angels for introductions
- target 10-15 high-quality investor meetings
- keep notes on objections
- revise narrative each week

### Weeks 7-8: Expand to funds

- start with the most relevant seed funds
- use intros wherever possible
- use Startup India Investor Connect as supplementary inbound/outbound support
- continue to improve materials based on repeated objections

### Weeks 9-10: Build momentum

- cluster meetings within 2 weeks
- share updates with interested investors
- send traction deltas
- convert "interesting but early" into "we are moving"

### Weeks 11-12: Close and narrow

- identify top 3-5 serious investors
- tighten round structure
- complete diligence materials
- keep other conversations warm until term clarity appears

## Outreach System

Create one simple pipeline spreadsheet.

Columns:

- investor name
- fund / role
- category: angel / operator / fund / accelerator / incubator
- thesis fit
- intro source
- date contacted
- status
- next step
- notes / objections

Statuses:

- not contacted
- intro requested
- intro sent
- meeting booked
- first meeting done
- follow-up sent
- diligence
- passed
- active

## Outreach Priorities

### Priority A

- fintech operators
- wealthtech angels
- consumer product angels
- founders who understand trust and retention

### Priority B

- seed funds with India fintech or consumer appetite
- accelerators with strong brand and network

### Priority C

- broader funds
- ecosystem channels
- cold outreach to larger VCs

## How To Ask For Intros

Keep intro requests short.

Example:

> I’m building SwingDesk, a research and analytics workbench for Indian active investors focused on unusual activity, screening, portfolio diagnostics, and backtesting. We’re building the software layer for self-directed users, not an advisory layer. I’m starting early fundraising conversations and would love an introduction to investors who understand Indian consumer fintech, wealthtech, or subscription software.

## What A Good First Round Looks Like

At this stage, the best fit is usually:

- angel round
- pre-seed round
- small seed round with operators plus one lead

Better to raise from:

- people who understand the category
- people who can help distribution and positioning
- people who will support a second round

Not just:

- the highest-visibility name
- the biggest fund that takes a call

## What To Track Weekly During Fundraising

- number of outreach messages sent
- number of warm intros requested
- number of meetings booked
- repeated objections
- deck iteration count
- traction changes
- new product milestones

## Decision Rule

If repeated investor feedback says:

- too broad
- unclear wedge
- unclear compliance story
- not enough retention proof

Then fix the product story first and keep fundraising narrower until the narrative sharpens.

## Practical Recommendations

1. Use Startup India for credibility, visibility, and ecosystem access.
2. Do not rely on Startup India alone for fundraising.
3. Start with operator-angels before institutional funds.
4. Raise on one sharp wedge, not the full feature list.
5. Keep the compliance story clean and proactive in every meeting.
